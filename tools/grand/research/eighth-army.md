# Eighth Army, 3 September – 31 December 1943

This note gives the British Eighth Army's order of battle and positions for the grand campaign. The playable area is
roughly 14.0°E to the Adriatic and 40.9°N to 42.5°N (Foggia, Lucera, Campobasso, Isernia, Castel di Sangro,
Ortona, Pescara). The note also covers where the formations came from: Calabria (Operation Baytown) and Taranto
(Operation Slapstick). The data file next to it is `eighth-army.json`, and it uses the same ids.

Every fact carries its source. **Suggested counter values are by analogy with the existing Salerno '43 and Italy
'43 counters. They are not from any rulebook.**

## Order of battle in outline

The army regrouped several times. Molony p.346 (3 Oct, "on paper"), p.454 (9 Oct), p.482n (Sangro) and p.595 (31 Dec)
give these outlines:

| date | 5th Corps (Allfrey) | 13th Corps (Dempsey) | Army troops |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Sep | — (HQ in Algeria) | 5 Div, 1 Cdn Div, 1 Cdn Army Tank Bde, 231 Bde, 3 Cdo, 40 RM Cdo, 1 SRS, 6 AGRA (Molony p.234n) | 1st Airborne Div under 15 Army Group at Taranto from 9 Sep (Nicholson p.222) |
| 3 Oct | 5 Div, 8 Ind Div, 1 Abn Div (in reserve at Taranto) | 78 Div, 1 Cdn Div + Army Tank Bde, 4 Armd Bde, SS Bde (Molony p.346, 433) | |
| 11 Oct | 78 Div, 8 Ind Div, 4 Armd Bde, 1 AGRA — coast to Larino | 5 Div, 1 Cdn Div, 1 Cdn Army Tank Bde, 6 AGRA — Larino to the Matese (Molony p.454-455) | |
| Sangro (late Nov) | 78 Div (11, 36, 38 Bdes) + 4 Armd Bde; 8 Ind Div (17, 21 Bdes); 1 Cdn Armd Bde in reserve from 15 Nov (Nicholson p.276) | 1 Cdn Div (1, 2, 3 Bdes); 5 Div (13, 15, 17 Bdes) + 12 Cdn Armd Regt | NZ Div (5, 6 Bdes, 4 NZ Armd Bde) + 19 Ind Bde under direct Army command (Molony p.482n) |
| 31 Dec | 1 Cdn Div, 8 Ind Div (right) | 5 Div, NZ Div, 78 Div (left) | 4 Ind Div, HQ 5 Cdn Armd Div (Molony p.595) |

**Battalions and regiments** (Molony p.117n, 152n, 234n, 433n, 454n, 495n, 500n):

- **1st Canadian Infantry Division** (Simonds; Vokes acting from 29 Sep to 15 Oct, then in command from 1 Nov). *1st Bde*: RCR, Hastings & Prince Edward Regt, 48th Highlanders. *2nd Bde*: PPCLI, Seaforth of Canada, Loyal Edmonton Regt. *3rd Bde*: Royal 22e Régt, Carleton & York, West Nova Scotia. Division troops: 4th Princess Louise Dragoon Guards (recce), 1st RCHA, 2nd and 3rd Fd Regts, 1st A/Tk, 2nd LAA, Saskatoon Light Infantry (MG).
- **1st Canadian Army Tank Brigade** (Wyman). Redesignated 1st Canadian Armoured Brigade on 26 Aug 1943; the new title was used in the field from November (Molony p.455n; Nicholson p.276n). Its regiments were the 11th (Ontario), 12th (Three Rivers) and 14th (Calgary).
- **5th Division** (Bucknall). *13th Bde*: 2 Cameronians, 2 Innisks, 2 Wilts. *15th Bde*: 1 Green Howards, 1 KOYLI, 1 Y&L. *17th Bde*: 2 RSF, 2 Northamptons, 6 Seaforth. Division troops: 5 Recce Regt; 91, 92, 156 Fd Regts; 52 A/Tk; 18 LAA; 7 Cheshire (MG).
- **78th Division** (Evelegh, then Keightley — Molony p.475). *11th Bde*: 2 LF, 1 Surreys, 5 Northamptons. *36th Bde*: 6 RWK, 5 Buffs, 8 A&SH. *38th (Irish) Bde*: 6 Innisks, 1 RIrF, 2 LIR. Division troops: 56 Recce Regt; 17, 132, 138 Fd Regts; 64 A/Tk; 49 LAA; 1 Kensingtons (MG).
- **8th Indian Division** (Russell). *17th Bde*: 1 R Fusiliers, 1/12 FFR, 1/5 RGR. *19th Bde*: 5 Essex, 3/8 Punjab, 6/13 FFRif. *21st Bde*: 5 RWK, 1/5 Mahratta LI, 3/15 Punjab. Division troops: 6th Lancers (recce); 3, 52, 53 Fd Regts RA; 26 LAA; 4 Mahratta A/Tk; 5 R Mahratta MG Bn (Molony p.454n; Tiger Triumphs ch.2).
- **2nd New Zealand Division** (Freyberg). *4 NZ Armd Bde*: 18, 19, 20 Armd Regts (Shermans), 22 Motor Bn. *5 Bde*: 21, 23, 28 (Maori) Bns. *6 Bde*: 24, 25, 26 Bns. Division troops: Div Cavalry (Staghounds); 4, 5, 6 Fd Regts; 14 LAA; 27 (MG) Bn. Molony says it had "six infantry battalions only and three armoured regiments" (p.495).
- **4th Armoured Brigade** (Currie). Its tank state on 25 Nov was 3 CLY 43 fit / 14 under repair, 44 RTR 53/3, 50 RTR 40/17, and 12 Cdn Armd Regt 50/3 (Molony p.483n). For the Termoli advance Molony lists "3rd County of London Yeomanry; 56th Reconnaissance Regiment" (p.434n). 46 RTR fought with 78 Div at San Salvo (Molony p.460); its parent formation is not stated.
- **Special Service Brigade** (Lt-Col Durnford-Slater): No. 3 Commando, 40 (RM) Commando and 1st Special Raiding Squadron (Molony p.346, 434n). **41 RM Commando was not at Termoli.** It is in the Salerno '43 counter mix with 2 Commando, from Salerno.
- **1st Airborne Division** (Hopkinson, killed at Castellaneta; then Down). It had the 1st, 2nd and 4th Parachute Brigades and the 1st Airlanding Brigade. The 4th Para Bde had two battalions, the 10th and 156th, with the third still forming. The Airlanding Bde was "understrength with only two battalions" (Wikipedia, Operation Slapstick). The 2nd Para Bde (4, 5, 6 Para Bns) stayed in Italy as an independent brigade group (Wikipedia, 2nd Parachute Brigade).
- **231st (Malta) Brigade** (Baytown only): 2 Devons, 1 Hampshires, 1 Dorsets, 165 Fd Regt (Molony p.234n).

## Period 1: Calabria and Taranto, 3–17 September (where the units came from)

- **3 Sep, Operation Baytown.** 13th Corps crossed the Strait of Messina. 1st Canadian Division assaulted with 3rd Bde between Reggio's northern suburbs and the Torbido. On its left 5th Division landed on two beaches, 13th Bde right and 17th Bde left. 1st Canadian Bde and 15th Bde followed up. The landing had the support of 630 guns, including 30th Corps artillery and 5th AGRA, plus three cruisers, five destroyers, three monitors, four gunboats and twenty landing craft (Molony p.234-235; Reggio 38.11N 15.66E).
- **4–10 Sep.** 5 Div went up the west coast (15 Bde on Highway 18 from 4 Sep). 231 Bde, 3 Cdo and 40 RM Cdo landed at Pizzo on 8 Sep. 5 Div's leading elements reached Nicastro on 10 Sep (Molony p.240-241; Nicholson p.220). The Canadians crossed the Aspromonte: 2 Bde was at Cittanova on 8 Sep, and "X Force" with the RCR reached Catanzaro on 10 Sep (Molony p.242; Nicholson p.220-221). Montgomery halted on the Catanzaro–Nicastro line on 10 Sep (Molony p.244).
- **9 Sep, Operation Slapstick.** 1st Airborne Division came by warship from Bizerta to Taranto, unopposed. The first lift was Div HQ and 1 and 4 Para Bde groups; the second lift was 2 Para Bde, 1 Airlanding Bde and the Glider Pilot Regt. HMS Abdiel struck a mine on 10 Sep, and 58 men of 6 Para Bn were drowned (Molony p.242-243; Wikipedia Operation Slapstick; Wikipedia 2nd Parachute Brigade). The division went through Massafra and Mottola to Castellaneta, where Hopkinson was killed, and entered Bari and Brindisi on 11 Sep (Molony p.243). By 13 Sep it held a perimeter 20 miles W and NW of Taranto (Nicholson p.222) and reached Gioia del Colle on 16–17 Sep (Wikipedia). HQ 5th Corps was at Taranto from 17 Sep (Molony p.346) and took command there on 18 Sep (Nicholson p.223).
- **11–17 Sep.** PLDG went to Crotone on 11 Sep (Nicholson p.222). On 16 Sep a PLDG patrol met a 1st Airborne patrol 40 miles SW of Taranto, and 5 Div was at Sapri, where its recce regiment met US 36 Div at Vallo (Nicholson p.224; Molony p.246-247). On the night of 17 Sep the Canadians were concentrated at Spezzano (1 Bde), Cassano (2 Bde) and Villapiana (3 Bde) (Nicholson p.223).

## Period 2: Potenza to Foggia, 17 September – 1 October

- **Potenza, 17–20 Sep.** "Boforce" was the West Novas, a Calgary squadron, a battery of 1 RCHA and detachments. It drove from Villapiana via Rotondella, Sant'Arcangelo, Corleto (18 Sep), Laurenzana and Anzi (19 Sep), and took Potenza on 20 Sep from a 100-strong battalion of 3 FJ Regt (Nicholson p.225-228; Molony p.344). 2 Cdn Bde rode through to cover Potenza on 21 Sep. 1 Cdn Bde, with the Calgaries (less a squadron) and PLDG, held the Scanzano plain between the Agri and Basento as a firm base (Nicholson p.228-229). 5 Div reached Auletta on 21 Sep (Molony p.344) and had touched the Canadians near Brienza on the evening of 20 Sep (Nicholson p.227-228). **On 21 Sep the Allied front ran continuously from Bari through Potenza to Salerno** (Nicholson p.229).
- **22–27 Sep, patrols to the Ofanto.** 1 FJ Division's screen ran from Atella to Spinazzola and on to Barletta (Nicholson p.231). The PPCLI held a base at Castello di Lagopesole and entered Rionero on 26 Sep. The R22eR found Spinazzola clear on 24 Sep. PLDG went to Canosa, and on 27 Sep PLDG and PPCLI patrols entered Melfi (Nicholson p.231-232).
- **Arrivals at the heel.** All dates are from Molony p.345-346 and its notes. 78 Div landed at Taranto: 11 Bde on 22 Sep, 36 Bde on 24 Sep, 38 Bde on 25 Sep. 4 Armd Bde landed on 23 Sep. 8 Ind Div from Egypt was "arriving from 23rd September", using Taranto and Brindisi; *Tiger Triumphs* ch.2 has six liners entering Taranto at dawn on "September 1?th" (the OCR digit is unreadable). The SS Brigade arrived on 28 Sep.
- **Foggia, 27 Sep.** 1 FJ Div fell back west and north of Foggia during the night of 26–27 Sep. On 27 Sep "Foggia was occupied by a force composed of 4th Armoured Brigade and some extra units" (Molony p.345). Nicholson p.232 says that elements of 78 Div and 4 Armd Bde, pushing up from Bari, crossed the Ofanto on 26 Sep and that "the 78th passed through it to the northern edge of the plain". Wikipedia (Operation Slapstick) says 1st Airborne reached Foggia with 78 Div; the official histories do not say so, so treat that as uncertain.
- **Positions about 30 Sep – 1 Oct** (Molony p.346, 433; Nicholson p.232, 235). 13th Corps concentrated in the Barletta–Bari–Gravina–Canosa quadrilateral. 1 Cdn Div was on the Canosa–Spinazzola road with the Army Tank Bde. The Ontarios' tanks were still at Scalea, and the Three Rivers were moving from Sicily to Manfredonia. 78 Div was on the right, with part of 4 Armd Bde patrolling beyond Foggia. Nicholson puts 78 Div, 4 Armd Bde and the SS Bde "along the coast between Barletta and Bari", which is the concentration area, not the advance. The SS Bde came under 13th Corps at Manfredonia on 30 Sep. 5 Div (5th Corps) was at Potenza, with orders to move to Foggia on 4 Oct. 1st Airborne was guarding the Foggia airfields, and 8 Ind Div was disembarking at Brindisi. On the German side, 1 FJ Div held a line from San Bartolomeo in Galdo to the coast at Termoli (Nicholson p.240).

## Period 3: Termoli and the Biferno, 1–15 October

**Plan** (Molony p.433). 1 Cdn Div was to go up Highway 17 on Vinchiaturo and Campobasso. The SS Bde was to take Termoli from the sea, followed by 36 and 38 Bdes, also by sea. 11 Bde and 4 Armd Bde were to go overland through Serracapriola. Montgomery expected to reach the Termoli–Campobasso line by 3 Oct and then pause 10–14 days for administration (Molony p.347).

**Termoli, 3–6 October** (Molony p.434-436; Nicholson p.251-255; Wikipedia Battle of Termoli):

- The SS Bde sailed from Manfredonia at midday on 2 Oct, landed at 2:15 a.m. on 3 Oct and scattered the 400-strong Battle Group Rau. The town and port were secured undamaged within six hours. Its patrols met 2 LF of 11 Bde on the Biferno that morning. 11 Bde crossed the Biferno "in penny packets" on 3 Oct, and heavy rain fell for 18 hours.
- 36 Bde landed during the night of 3–4 Oct. On 4 Oct 16 Pz Div's battle groups arrived: Stempel at Petacciato and von Doering at San Giacomo. The Bailey bridging did not arrive until 4 Oct.
- 5 Oct was the crisis. About 20 Pz IV of 2 Pz Regt joined the attack. The Argylls (162 casualties) were pushed back from the Valentino brickworks, and the RWK and Buffs were driven off the Coccia ridge. 214 Fd Coy's Bailey bridge opened at 3 p.m., and 3 CLY crossed and counter-attacked. Two squadrons of the Three Rivers arrived by forced march at Campomarino and Termoli, and 38 Bde landed in the evening. US XII ASC fighter-bombers were diverted to help the Desert Air Force.
- On 6 Oct the counter-attack began to turn the battle by noon: the RIrF and 6 Innisks took San Giacomo, and the LIR cleared to the Sinarca. Herr allowed a withdrawal at 4:35 p.m. Each side lost "well over five hundred" men. 78 Div pushed on to Larino and towards Petacciato on 7–8 Oct.

**Canadians to Campobasso** (Nicholson ch. IX; Molony p.436-437):

- 1 Oct: the advanced guard (RCR, Calgaries, PLDG) left Lucera. The RCR took Motta in the night of 1–2 Oct, and the ridge was cleared by 3 Oct.
- 1 Bde: Volturara on 3 Oct, San Marco la Catola on 4 Oct, Mt Miano on 6 Oct. Carlantino and Celenza Valfortore were found clear.
- 3 Bde relieved 1 Bde on 6 Oct and took Gambatesa on 7–8 Oct, then Jelsi, Mt Verdone and Gildone on 9–12 Oct. PLDG entered Colletorto on 8 Oct.
- 2 Bde on the left went by Castelfranco, Montefalcone and Foiano (raid on Baselice 5 Oct; contact with US 45 Div from Foiano 6 Oct), then Decorata (8 Oct), Castelpagano (9 Oct) and Santa Croce (12 Oct).
- Campobasso was taken without a fight on 14 Oct. La Rocca fell on 14 Oct and the Vinchiaturo road fork on 15 Oct.
- The opponents were Battle Group Heilmann of 1 FJ Div (3 FJ Regt plus a battalion of 1 FJ Regt, about 1,350 men), then 29 PG Div and 67 PG Regt of 26 Pz Div (Nicholson p.240-241; Molony p.437).
- The 1st Airlanding Light Regt RA (75 mm pack guns) was attached to the Canadians from 5 Oct (Nicholson p.243).

## Period 4: regroup, the Biferno west bank, the Trigno and Isernia, 15 October – 9 November

- **Administration and HQs.** Railheads opened at San Severo (5th Corps) and Lucera (13th Corps) on 30 Oct, and Foggia became the Army railhead. Forward maintenance centres were 110 Foggia, 111 Lucera and 112 Campobasso (13th Corps), and 200 Trani, 201 Termoli and 202 Larino (5th Corps). Main HQ Eighth Army was near Lucera and Rear HQ at Mottola. HQ 15th Army Group was at S. Spirito near Bari (Molony p.455-456n).
- **11 Oct.** 5th Corps took the coast–Larino sector and 13th Corps the sector from Larino to the Matese (Molony p.455).
- **8 Ind Div.** 17 Ind Bde relieved a British brigade at Larino on 18 Oct (*Tiger Triumphs* ch.2).
- **13th Corps, west of the Biferno** (Nicholson p.255-263; Molony p.458):
  - Montagano was entered on 14 Oct; Baranello on 17–18 Oct; Busso on 19 Oct; Oratino and San Stefano on 20 Oct; Guardiaregia on 18 Oct; Campochiaro on 21 Oct.
  - Boiano fell on 24 Oct to the Carleton & Yorks. Colle d'Anchise (Edmontons) and Spinete (PPCLI) were taken on 23–24 Oct, Castropignano and Roccaspromonte on 24 Oct, and Torella and Molise by 27 Oct.
  - 5 Div moved over from the right to the Vinchiaturo area (Nicholson p.259). It relieved the Canadians at Boiano and Spinete on 27–28 Oct and advanced on Isernia with a brigade on each side of the road. 13 Bde patrols entered an empty Isernia on 4 Nov.
  - Canadian outposts reached Frosolone (29 Oct), Duronia and Pietracupa (4 Nov) and later Salcito. PLDG filled the gap between the Biferno and the Trigno from the Montagano–Petrella area (Nicholson p.263-264).
- **Trigno** (Molony p.456-461):
  - Light forces of 5th Corps cleared the approaches between 15 and 22 Oct. By 27 Oct 78 Div had a narrow bridgehead, with 11 Bde near the coast and 38 Bde inland. 38 Bde's attack on San Salvo in the night of 27–28 Oct was called off.
  - 19 Ind Bde closed up at Montemitro and Montefalcone, and 21 Ind Bde moved towards Roccavivara. Rain postponed the attack from 29 Oct to 2–3 Nov.
  - 8 Ind Div attacked first, in the night of 1–2 Nov: 19 Bde against Tufillo and Mt Farano (held by most of 3 FJ Regt), which were taken early on 5 Nov. 21 Bde reached Celenza.
  - 78 Div attacked at 4:30 a.m. on 3 Nov with 36 Bde plus 6 Innisks and two squadrons of 46 RTR, and took San Salvo and the Buonanotte canal. 11 Bde attacked San Salvo station. Destroyers *Queenborough* and *Raider* bombarded Vasto to fake a landing. 16 Pz Div withdrew towards the Advanced Sangro Line in the night of 3–4 Nov.
  - **By 9 Nov** 78 Div was on the Sangro between Paglieta and Mt Calvo; 11 Bde had reached them on 7 Nov with two 4 Armd Bde squadrons (p.485). 19 Ind Bde was south-west of Atessa, 17 Ind Bde at Gissi, and 21 Ind Bde at Torrebruna–Castiglione (p.461).

## Period 5: the Sangro, 9 November – 1 December

- **Plan** (Molony p.481-487). The main attack was to be by 5th Corps between Mozzagrogna and Fossacesia. The NZ Division, under Army command, was to strike through Atessa and Casoli for Guardiagrele and Chieti. 13th Corps was to stage a diversion on Alfedena and Castel di Sangro. Deception included false dumps, Urdu wireless traffic, and a naval demonstration towards Pescara with fake preparations by 1st Airborne (Nicholson p.276-277). On 26 Nov the army's artillery was 22 field and 7 medium regiments, 690 guns (Molony p.482n).
- **NZ Division arrival** (Phillips p.37-46; Molony p.486). The first flight (5,827 men) landed at Taranto on 9 Oct; the second (8,707) sailed on 18 Oct. Troops bivouacked north of Taranto, and the first vehicles reached Bari on 29 Oct. The move to an assembly area west of Lucera began on 1 Nov. On 11 Nov the division was ordered forward to Furci–Gissi, and Div HQ opened south of Gissi on 14 Nov. It took 19 Ind Bde under command on 14 Nov.
- **19 Ind Bde with the NZ Division.** With 19 NZ Armd Regt it took Perano on 17–18 Nov and Archi on 20 Nov, and forded the Sangro in the night of 22–23 Nov (Molony p.486; Phillips p.49-50). The "hump" between the Sangro and the Aventino and Altino were held by 25 Nov (Phillips p.48, 59, 63). NZ 6 Bde entered the line in the night of 19–20 Nov, and both NZ brigades were in by 25 Nov (Phillips p.48, 54).
- **13th Corps diversion** (Nicholson p.277-285; Molony p.485):
  - PLDG had a patrol in Carovilli from 8 Nov. On 16 Nov 3 Cdn Bde's HQ was at Carovilli and tactical Div HQ at Pescolanciano.
  - Vastogirardi was reached on 17–18 Nov and San Pietro Avellana on 20 Nov. Capracotta was reached, and patrols crossed the Sangro between Sant'Angelo and Ateleta.
  - The West Novas attacked Point 1009 on 23–24 Nov, with a nine-regiment artillery demonstration on 24 Nov. The Castel di Sangro attack was called off.
  - 5 Div took Mt Civitalta, attacked Alfedena on 22 Nov and had cleared it by early 23 Nov.
  - From 26 Nov 5 Div relieved 3 Cdn Bde.
- **Bridgeheads.** 78 Div's patrols crossed nightly from 10 Nov. By 22 Nov 11 Bde had a bridgehead east of Fossacesia and 36 Bde one west of it, with six battalions and some tanks of 3 CLY and 50 RTR on the escarpment (Molony p.485-486).
- **Floods.** D-day, set for the night of 19–20 Nov, was postponed. A flood in the night of 22–23 Nov stranded the bridges, and work stopped until 26 Nov (Molony p.487-488).
- **The battle** (Molony p.488-491):
  - 27 Nov: 1/12 FFR took the jumping-off place below Mozzagrogna, and 38 Bde crossed. That evening 1/5 RGR attacked Mozzagrogna and reported it taken at about 2 a.m. on 28 Nov.
  - 28 Nov: a counter-attack by 26 Pz Div at dawn caused a withdrawal. By evening 124 tanks of 4 Armd Bde were across.
  - 29 Nov: Mozzagrogna was retaken by 1:30 a.m. 3 CLY and 6 Innisks cleared S. Maria, and NZ 5 and 6 Bdes took the high ground north of the river.
  - 30 Nov: 3 CLY, 44 RTR and 2 LIR took Fossacesia.
  - 1 Dec: the LIR and 6 Innisks took Rocca. 24 NZ Bn was overlooking Castelfrentano from the evening of 30 Nov.
  - In Molony's words, "By 1st December the 8th Army ... had broken the Adriatic end of the Bernhardt Line" (p.491). Eighth Army's November casualties were 2,842 (p.491n).

## Period 6: the Moro, Ortona and Orsogna, 1–31 December

**Reliefs:**

- 1 Cdn Div moved from 13th Corps to relieve 78 Div. 2 Cdn Bde left Campobasso on 30 Nov for a staging area round San Salvo and Petacciato, and 1 Bde followed on 1 Dec.
- Late on 1 Dec 2 Cdn Bde crossed and relieved 11 Bde south of Fossacesia. Command passed to the Canadians on 2 Dec. 38 Bde and 4 Armd Bde stayed under Canadian command (Nicholson p.288-289; Molony p.494, 500).
- 1 Cdn Armd Bde (in 5th Corps reserve since 15 Nov) came forward. The Calgaries crossed on 1 Dec under 8 Ind Div, carried Sikh troops to Lanciano in the night of 2–3 Dec and were at Frisa on 4 Dec (Nicholson p.288-290).

**Coast** (Molony p.500-507; Nicholson ch. X-XI):

- 38 Bde and 4 Armd Bde took San Vito on 3 Dec and reached the Moro on 4 Dec. 21 Ind Bde took Lanciano on 3 Dec and Frisa on 5 Dec.
- On 5 Dec 2 Cdn Bde was near Sant'Apollinare, 1 Bde near San Vito, and 3 Bde was trying to cross the flooded Sangro. On 4 Dec a six-foot spate carried away most of the Sangro bridges.
- 5–6 Dec: the PPCLI took Villa Rogatti and the Seaforth got two companies across at San Leonardo, but both were back on their own bank by evening. The Hastings kept a bridgehead near the coast, and 21 Ind Bde took over Villa Rogatti on 7–8 Dec.
- 8–9 Dec: the RCR "right hook" and the 48th on La Torre, then the Seaforth and Calgaries took San Leonardo at 5:40 p.m. on 9 Dec. The Calgaries lost 27 of 51 tanks. Bengal Sappers built the "Impossible Bridge". "8th Army had crossed the Moro along its length" (p.502).
- 1 Cdn Armd Bde relieved 4 Armd Bde at 6 p.m. on 8 Dec; 44 RTR stayed under Wyman until the Three Rivers returned on 15 Dec (Nicholson p.298, 317-318).
- **The Gully, 10–19 Dec** (over 1,000 Canadian casualties): eight main attacks. Casa Berardi was taken by Capt. Triquet's company of the R22eR with Ontario tanks on 14 Dec. The 18–19 Dec attack ("Morning Glory") by the 48th, RCR, Hastings and Three Rivers took the Gully and Cider crossroads by dusk on 19 Dec.
- 8 Ind Div took Villa Caldari on 14 Dec and Villa Jubatti on 17 Dec, and patrolled into Crecchio on 18 Dec (Nicholson p.322).
- **Ortona, 21–28 Dec:** the Edmontons and Three Rivers tanks went in on 21 Dec and the Seaforth on 23 Dec. The PPCLI were committed on 27 Dec. The parachutists left during the night of 27–28 Dec, and the town was clear on 28 Dec, at a cost of 650 Canadian casualties (Molony p.507; Nicholson p.323-333). The defence was by II/3, III/3 and II/4 FJ Regts.
- **Villa Grande, 22–28 Dec:** 19 Ind Bde (1/5 Essex, 3/8 Punjab) fought for the village from 22 Dec; it was occupied on 28 Dec. The Essex lost 285 men and were withdrawn to re-form (Molony p.506; *Tiger Triumphs*).
- **1 Cdn Bde from 23 Dec** went for San Nicola and San Tommaso; the 48th took them on 31 Dec. 3 Cdn Bde attacked along the coast from 29 Dec. Point 59 near Torre Mucchia fell on 4 Jan (Nicholson p.333-338; Molony p.507).

**Inland** (Molony p.494-500, 506-507; Phillips p.80-150):

- NZ Div entered Castelfrentano on 2 Dec. 24 Bn reached Brecciarola ridge, and 25 Bn broke into Orsogna at dawn on 3 Dec but was repulsed (83 casualties). The motor battalion was held at Melone.
- 2nd Independent Para Bde, as Army troops under the NZ Division, relieved the NZ at Casoli on the evening of 5 Dec. 6 Para Bn relieved 22 Bn facing the Melone road fork (Phillips p.105-106; Molony p.498).
- 7 Dec: the divisional attack on Orsogna failed. 23 Bn kept a lodgement on Sfasciata.
- 12 Dec: 17 Bde of 5 Div came under NZ command to fill the gap to the Indians. HQ 13th Corps took over on 15 Dec. 5 Div was in line on the NZ right in the night of 15–16 Dec, having been relieved in the mountains by 78 Div, and entered Poggiofiorito on 17 Dec (Phillips p.119, 133; Molony p.497, 505; Nicholson p.322).
- 15–16 Dec: the NZ attack gained the road and the ridges but not Orsogna.
- 23–25 Dec: the 13th Corps attack, with 5 Div towards Arielli and NZ 5 Bde (plus 26 Bn) on Fontegrande, failed to break through. NZ held the first Fontegrande ridge ("Jittery Ridge") about 300 yards from the enemy (Phillips p.140-147; Nicholson p.338).
- 27–28 Dec: 13 Bde relieved 2 Para Bde of the Aventino sector including Casoli. The NZ were now flanked by 15 Bde (Poggiofiorito) on the right and 13 Bde on the left (Phillips p.150).

**Command change.** On 30 Dec Montgomery handed over to Leese, and the policy became containment (Molony p.509-510). Eighth Army's casualties in December were 6,453 (p.509). The Canadian Division lost 176 officers and 2,163 other ranks between the Moro and the Riccio (Nicholson p.338). Molony gives 2,339 Canadian, 3,400 8th Indian and 1,200 NZ casualties for the Sangro and after (p.507); Wikipedia's Moro River campaign article gives 1,600 NZ for 9 Nov–31 Dec, citing Phillips.

### The line on 31 December 1943

From the coast inland:

- **Coast:** 3 Cdn Bde was attacking along the coast NW of Ortona, short of Torre Mucchia; Point 59 fell on 4 Jan. 2 Cdn Bde was in and beyond Ortona, cleared on 28 Dec.
- **Riccio:** 1 Cdn Bde held San Nicola and San Tommaso, taken on 31 Dec.
- **Villa Grande:** 8 Ind Div had held Villa Grande since 28 Dec. *German-held:* Crecchio and Arielli.
- **Poggiofiorito sector:** 5 Div's 15 Bde.
- **North of Orsogna:** NZ Div held Fontegrande ("Jittery Ridge") with 6 Bde and 6 Para Bn on the Fontegrande–Orsogna sub-sector. *German-held:* Orsogna and Guardiagrele.
- **Salarola:** 4 Para Bn faced the Melone road fork.
- **Reserve:** NZ 5 Bde on the Castelfrentano ridge.
- **Bianco–Barone:** a company of 5 Para Bn and two Div Cavalry squadrons on the steep south-western flank.
- **Casoli and the Aventino:** 5 Div's 13 Bde.
- **Upper Sangro mountains:** 78 Div in 13th Corps. Early in January it extended right to the Casoli area.

Sources: Nicholson p.336-338; Phillips p.147-150; Molony p.595.

The Germans had planned to fall back from Ortona to the Tesoro stream, from west of Villa Grande to Torre Mucchia, while holding from near Crecchio to Orsogna (Molony p.506). Wikipedia (Moro River campaign) says 5 Div took Arielli. That conflicts with Nicholson p.338, which says "The year ended with the Germans still in possession of Crecchio, Arielli, Orsogna and Guardiagrele"; follow Nicholson.

## Artillery

- **Baytown.** 13th Corps had 6 AGRA and 2 AA Bde, with 5 AGRA and 30th Corps artillery in support: 630 guns under Brig. Dennis. 15th Army Group RA included four American 155 mm battalions (Molony p.234-235).
- **From 9 Oct.** 5th Corps had 1 AGRA and 13th Corps 6 AGRA (Molony p.454).
- **Trigno.** 8 Ind Div had four field and one medium regiment. 78 Div had three extra field and three medium regiments, with ammunition at 500 rounds per gun for field and 475 for medium (Molony p.459-460).
- **Sangro.** The 5th Corps grouping was 1 and 6 AGRA (51, 66, 70, 80 Medium Regts). 78 Div had 17, 57, 98, 132 and 138 Fd; 8 Ind Div had 52, 53, 166 Fd and one more regiment whose number is unreadable in the OCR (Molony p.490n). The NZ Div had 6 AGRA with it: 66 and 80 Medium, 11 and 117 Fd (Molony p.495n). This overlaps with the 5th Corps list, presumably because units moved.
- **Moro, 8 Dec.** Canadian division artillery plus 57 and 98 Fd and 4 and 70 Medium (Molony p.501n); 98 Army Fd Regt (SP) (Nicholson p.298n). For "Morning Glory" there were 4, 58 and 70 Medium among others (Nicholson p.316n).
- **13th Corps, upper Sangro, 24 Nov.** 75 Medium, 78 and 165 Fd under corps command (Nicholson p.284n).

## Air and naval

- **Desert Air Force.** The DAF, with the NATBF light and medium bombers, gave the direct support. Advanced HQ DAF moved from Minervino to Lucera on 11 Oct (Molony p.461).
- **NATAF order of battle, 4 Nov** (Molony p.462-463):
  - DAF Advanced and Rear HQ in the Lucera area.
  - No. 239 Wing (6 sqns Kittyhawks) and No. 244 Wing (4 sqns Spitfires).
  - US 57th Group (Amendola) and US 79th Group, both Kittyhawks.
  - No. 7 SAAF Wing (3 sqns Spitfires, Palata) and No. 285 Wing (Tac R and PR, Foggia).
  - NATBF Advanced HQ at Segezia.
  - The table lists Kittyhawk and Spitfire squadrons at Termoli as well.
- **Peak air efforts.**
  - Termoli: on 5 Oct British and American fighter-bombers flew 418 sorties, and on 6 Oct DAF fighter-bombers flew over 400 (Molony p.436n).
  - Trigno, 2 and 3 Nov: 585 and 616 sorties, "the best air support 8th Army enjoyed" in that period (Molony p.459n).
  - Sangro: Molony p.492 gives a day-by-day summary for 20–30 Nov.
  - Moro, 8 Dec: 108 fighter-bomber and 72 light-bomber sorties on the Canadian front (Molony p.501).
  - Ortona was deliberately spared from bombing (Molony p.509).
- **RCAF.** 417 (City of Windsor) Squadron's Spitfires patrolled over the Sangro bridges (Nicholson p.288).
- **Naval.**
  - Baytown fire support (Molony p.235).
  - Slapstick was carried in cruisers and a minelayer (Nicholson p.222).
  - The Termoli landing sailed from Manfredonia (Nicholson p.252).
  - At the Trigno, destroyers *Queenborough* and *Raider* staged a feint landing at Vasto (Molony p.460).
  - For the Sangro there was a naval demonstration towards Pescara (Molony p.485).
  - DUKWs ran supplies from Termoli and Vasto (Molony p.460n, 488).
  - The air raid on Bari of 2–3 Dec is in Molony ch. XV; it was not read here. Italy '43 marks it on turn 23.

## Opponents, for context (76th Panzer Corps, Herr)

- **1 Fallschirmjäger Div (Heidrich).** In Apulia it had a fighting strength of about 1,300 (Nicholson p.231). It then held the Biferno–Fortore front (Battle Group Heilmann), the Trigno at Tufillo, and the upper Sangro. From mid-December it moved to Ortona (Molony p.502).
- **16 Pz Div.** At Termoli, then on the Trigno (San Salvo).
- **26 Pz Div and 29 PG Div.** Opposite the Canadians in October. 26 Pz Div came back to the Sangro and Orsogna.
- **65 Inf Div.** Held the Sangro line and was broken there.
- **90 PG Div.** At the Moro from 3 December.

Strengths on 4 Nov (Molony p.457n): 16 Pz 4,598; 1 FJ 4,424; 26 Pz 5,390; 65 ID 6,552.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- **1st Airborne Division.** I found no exact date for its departure from Italy ("December", Wikipedia), and its brigades' positions after 1 Oct are not given.
- **Special Service Brigade.** Where it went after Termoli is not given in the sources read.
- **4th Armoured Brigade.** Its full regimental composition at Termoli and the Trigno is not given (Molony names only 3 CLY and 56 Recce for Termoli). The date it left for the UK is not established. 46 RTR's parent formation is unknown.
- **8th Indian Division.** Its exact landing date is unclear (Molony "from 23rd September"; *Tiger Triumphs* OCR "September 1?th"). The date 19 Ind Bde returned to the division from the NZ is not given.
- **78th Division in December.** Its exact sector in the upper Sangro after relieving 5 Div (mid-Dec) is not given by place. The JSON puts it at Castel di Sangro and flags that as uncertain.
- **Hamlets.** Many places have no Wikipedia coordinates: San Leonardo, Villa Rogatti, Casa Berardi, the Cider crossroads, Villa Grande, Villa Caldari and Villa Jubatti, San Nicola and San Tommaso, Torre Mucchia, Melone, Fontegrande, Mt Farano and Point 1009. They carry approximate positions estimated from the texts' descriptions and are flagged `uncertain`.
- **Brigade positions.** Molony and Nicholson usually give division-level positions; JSON position entries marked "division-level position" are of that kind.
- **German units.** These are only summarised; this note is about the Eighth Army.
